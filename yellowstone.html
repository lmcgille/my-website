Yellowstone

The World's First National Park

Established in 1872, Yellowstone National Park is a nearly 3,500-sq.-mile wilderness recreation area atop a volcanic hot spot. Mostly in Wyoming, the park spreads into Montana and Idaho too. Yellowstone features dramatic canyons, alpine rivers, lush forests, hot springs and gushing geysers, including its most famous, Old Faithful.

One of the park's most stunning features is the Grand Prismatic Spring. As the largest hot spring in the United States, it is famous for its striking rainbow-like colors, caused by pigmented thermophilic bacteria that thrive in the mineral-rich waters. The spring is deeper than a 10-story building and produces over 500 gallons of hot water every minute.

Fun Facts

  • Yellowstone is home to more than half of the world's geysers.
  • The park sits on top of an active supervolcano, which last erupted 640,000 years ago.
  • There are over 10,000 hydrothermal features in the park, including mud pots and fumaroles.
  • Yellowstone is the only place in the U.S. where bison have lived continuously since prehistoric times.

It's also home to hundreds of animal species, including bears, wolves, bison, elk and antelope. The park's varied ecosystems provide endless opportunities for hiking, camping, and wildlife photography. Whether you're watching the sunset over the Grand Canyon of the Yellowstone or waiting for a geyser to erupt, the park offers a unique glimpse into the raw power of nature.
