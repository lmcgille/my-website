Mount Desert Island, Maine · Est. 1919

Acadia

National Park

The Crown Jewel of the North Atlantic Coast

Acadia National Park is a 47,000-acre Atlantic coast recreation area primarily on Maine's Mount Desert Island. Its landscape is marked by woodland, rocky beaches and glacier-scoured granite peaks such as Cadillac Mountain, the highest point on the United States’ East Coast.

The park features an extensive system of Carriage Roads—45 miles of crushed-stone roads built by John D. Rockefeller Jr. These roads were designed for horses and carriages, but today they are popular for biking and hiking, offering a unique way to see the park's scenic beauty without cars.

Fun Facts

  • Between October and March, Cadillac Mountain is the first place in the U.S. to see the sunrise.
  • Thunder Hole is a small inlet where the waves crash in with a thunderous boom.
  • The park was originally established as Lafayette National Park in 1919.
  • Acadia is one of the top ten most visited national parks in the United States.

Among the wildlife are moose, bear, whales and seabirds. The park includes mountains, an ocean coastline, woodlands, and lakes. In addition to Mount Desert Island, the park also contains much of the Isle au Haut and the Schoodic Peninsula.
