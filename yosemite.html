Yosemite

National Park

A Temple of Nature

Yosemite National Park is in California’s Sierra Nevada mountains. It’s famed for its giant, ancient sequoia trees, and for Tunnel View, the iconic vista of towering Bridalveil Fall and the granite cliffs of El Capitan and Half Dome.

The park was a key inspiration for John Muir, known as the "Father of the National Parks," whose writings helped preserve Yosemite Valley and the surrounding wilderness. His passion for the Sierra Nevada helped spark the American conservation movement.

Fun Facts

  • Yosemite Falls is the tallest waterfall in North America, dropping a total of 2,425 feet.
  • El Capitan is one of the world's largest monoliths of exposed granite, rising 3,000 feet from the valley floor.
  • The Giant Sequoias in the Mariposa Grove can live for over 3,000 years.
  • In February, the Horsetail Fall can appear to "catch fire" during sunset, creating a rare phenomenon known as the Firefall.

In Yosemite Village are shops, restaurants, lodging, the Yosemite Museum and the Ansel Adams Gallery, with prints of the photographer’s renowned black-and-white landscapes of the area. The park's diverse landscapes range from high-altitude meadows to deep valleys, offering some of the most spectacular scenery in the world.
